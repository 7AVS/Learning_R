1 - Executive Summary

  • Why are we testing? The async banner is a new program: serving NBA offers as banners inside the mobile app, a channel the bank has not used for these campaigns before. Before the channel becomes part of standard deployment, we need causal proof that it produces conversions the existing experience does not.
  • What is being tested and measured? The channel itself, with O2P (pre-approved overdraft) as the first offer through it. Challenger = the client's mobile experience with the O2P banner. Champion = the same client experience without it. Measured: overdraft conversion within the deployment window.
  • What will success look like? A statistically significant conversion lift in the banner arm, stable across deployment cohorts. That result makes the channel BAU (see the follow-on 5% holdout DOE).
  • How long and how safe? Three deployment cohorts (Jun 18, Jul 7, Aug 11), each read at a 30-day window; final read ~Sep 11. Safe by construction: the banner adds a surface, removes nothing, and both arms keep every other channel their leaf allows.
  • Interim answer: +0.19 to +0.20pp lift (+31% relative), consistent across the two cohorts read so far.

2 - Strategic Context

The async banner capability shipped under NBA-12268 with three campaigns (PCD, CTU, O2P). O2P is the campaign where the channel gets a clean causal test. A simple before/after or served/not-served comparison would not work here: clients who see mobile banners are mobile- active, and mobile-active clients convert more with or without a banner. Self-selection would inflate any observational read. The champion/challenger design removes that bias by randomizing banner exposure within the same decisioned Action population, so the two arms differ in nothing but the banner. The 50/50 split maximizes power for a first read of an unproven channel. This experiment is the template for onboarding the async channel to other campaigns: prove the channel, then shrink the holdout and make it BAU.

3 - Hypothesis & Objectives

  • Primary (ATE): τ = E[Y(MB) − Y(NON_MB)], Y = overdraft conversion in the window. H0: τ = 0 vs H1: τ > 0.
  • Success criteria: reject H0 at α = 0.05 (two-sided z-test) at the full window, with lift directionally consistent across cohorts.
  • Secondary: lift stability across cohorts (channel durability, creative fatigue) and across segments PR / NL / OT where volumes allow.
  • Metrics: Primary = responders_target per the Async_tracker [OPEN: confirm whether this counts product 43 only (primary success) or 40/41/43 (secondary)].
  • Guard rail: none defined at launch (template default unsubscribe is email-specific).

4 - Experiment Design & Treatment Assignment

  • Design type: two-proportion z-test. Chosen because the outcome is binary (converted or not), arms are independent, and n per arm (>230K) puts the normal approximation beyond question. No stratification was needed: full randomization at this n balances covariates.
  • Randomization unit: client (clnt_no), randomized within TG4 at cohort assignment.
  • Eligibility: O2P decisioned Action population, banner-capable report-group leaves 01 / 03 / 07 in the PR (Premium) / NL (No Limit) / OT (Other) trees (tech spec §6.3, o2p_offer_channel_trees.md). Tactic IDs 2026099O2P / 2026126O2P / 2026132O2P.
  • Treatment arms (split tree, share of cohort):
Deployment cohort (~505K decisioned clients)
├─ TG4 Action 95%
│   ├─ MB (challenger): mobile banner served ..... 47.5%
│   └─ NON_MB (champion): no banner ............... 47.5%
└─ TG7 campaign control (never in channel) ........ 5%

TG7 measures the whole campaign vs nothing and is outside this contrast.

5 - Sample Size & Power Analysis

  • Baseline conversion rate: no channel-specific baseline existed before launch (new channel); the 50/50 split was sized for maximum power rather than to a target MDE. Realized NON_MB baseline: 0.628% (average of Jun 18 short-window 0.633% and Jul 7 full 0.623%).
  • Power calculation steps (post-hoc, per cohort):
  • n per arm ≈ 253K (smallest cohort), p = 0.00628.
  • SE = √(p(1−p)(1/n₁+1/n₂)) = √(0.00624 × 2/253,000) ≈ 0.000222.
  • MDE = (z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈₀) × SE = 2.80 × 0.000222 ≈ 0.062pp.
  • Observed lift +0.198pp is 3.2× the MDE; power to detect it ≈ 100%.
  • Final sample size: one cohort was already sufficient; three cohorts were run to test stability, not to reach significance. The surplus power is what justifies the follow-on move to a 4.75% holdout (o2p_async_holdout_doe_draft.md, o2p_holdout_mde_calculator.xlsx).

7 - Randomization & Quality Checks

  • SRM (chi-square, Cohen effective size): expected 50/50 within TG4. Observed arm gaps: 6 clients (Jun 18), 3 (Jul 7), 4 (Aug 11). Cohen w = √(χ²/n) < 0.0001 in every cohort, far under the 0.05 threshold. No SRM.

8 - Risk & Mitigation (learned during execution)

  • Tracker arm labels champion/challenger are inverted. All reporting uses MB / NON_MB.
  • %IM% is NOT a mobile filter (it matches the IN_IM OLB eOffer token; also a CRV quirk). Mobile = MB token. Legacy O2P daily trackers still carry the IM filter; patch before reusing their outputs.
  • Jun 18 short window: exclude from steady-state rate claims; keep in pooled lift reads only with the window noted.
