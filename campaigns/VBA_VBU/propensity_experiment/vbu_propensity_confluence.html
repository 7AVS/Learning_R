1 - Executive Summary

At-a-glance: we hold out a random 30% of the clients the VBU propensity model selects for the AIB upgrade offers and send them nothing, to prove the model-targeted communication causes the upgrades and to measure that value by score band.

  • Why are we testing? The model path is live BAU (running from before our data window; earliest wave in our probes: 2026-04-13) with no causal value number. The existing ~5% not-communicated group is not a verified random control (its share varies by score band), so today's numbers can't certify the model.
  • What is being tested and measured? Target-product upgrade rate, communicated vs held-out, per offer — overall and by model score band.
  • What will success look like? Treatment converts at the historical ~1.79% (NR) / ~2.40% (R_55) with holdout ≈ 0, and the lift concentrates in the top score bands (band 1: ~3.4–7.9pp).
  • How long and how safe? Two monthly waves, each read ~80 days post-deploy; reversible at any wave boundary. Cost: ~100–160 forgone upgrades per wave at 70/30.

2 - Hypothesis & Objectives

  • Primary Hypothesis: communicating the model's selections causes upgrades. τ = E[Y(Comm) − Y(No touch)], Y = target-product upgrade within the ~80-day response window. H0: τ = 0; H1: τ > 0 (one-sided). Read separately per offer (NR, R_55).
  • Secondary Hypothesis: causal lift declines monotonically with model score band (band 1 ≈ 3.4–7.9pp fading to ~0 by band 5) — the calibration read that validates the model's ranking and cutoff placement.
  • Metrics:
    • Primary: target-product upgrade rate
    • Secondary: any-product upgrade rate; lift by score band
    • Guard Rail: email unsubscribe rate

3 - Experiment Design & Treatment Assignment

  • Design Type: RCT; one-sided exact test (Fisher) rather than Z-test, because the control arm is expected at ~0 conversions (0 of 2,956+ historical not-communicated) — the normal approximation is invalid at zero counts.
  • Randomization Unit: client. Single client-level random draw across the whole model-based population — no band logic needed in decisioning; scores and bands are stored for BOTH arms at assignment, so every score band inherits its own ~70/30 split and results are read per band.
  • Eligibility Rules: clients selected by the propensity model for a model-based offer (AIB_25K_NR or AIB_25K_R_55) in the wave; CIDM waterfall unchanged upstream of the split.
  • Treatment Arms (split tree, ratio 70/30):

4 - Sample Size & Power Analysis

  • Baseline Conversion Rate: NR 1.79%, R_55 2.40% — communicated clients, mature June/July 2026 waves, curated conversion table joined to tactic populations (clnt_no + tactic_id), ~80-day response windows; stable across both waves. Assumption for the holdout side: ≈ 0 (0/2,956 historical not-communicated; ~0 organic any-product movement).
  • Power Analysis & MDE: with a ~2% vs ~0 contrast this is a detection problem, not a classic MDE: the headline is overpowered at any holdout ≥ 10%. Holdout size is set by score-band depth. Detection rule (exact test, α=0.05 one-sided, 80% power): conversions needed per band ≈ 5 at 50% holdout, 9 at 30%, 14 at 20%.

5 - Analysis Plan and Results

  • Randomization & Quality Checks:
    • SRM — chi-square on the day-1 assignment file, overall AND per score band (the per-band check verifies the draw was score-independent; the current BAU holdout fails this pattern, which is why the experiment needs a fresh draw).
    • Covariate Balance (SMD method) — SMD on model score, treatment vs holdout (both arms carry scores; investigate if SMD > 0.1).
  • Primary Test Results: Interim = wave 1 at ~80 days post-deploy; Final = 2 waves pooled. If O'Brien-Fleming spending is required: interim α = 0.005, final α = 0.048 — immaterial here given the overpowered headline.
