How consent and email connect today

Two systems that were never wired together

When a client manages their marketing consent, that lives in the bank’s Consent Preference Centre. When an email actually gets sent, that happens in the vendor email platform. These are two separate systems — and a decision in one does not automatically reach the other.

Consent Preference Centre
Vendor email platform
0.3%

of email unsubscribes ever show up as a change in the bank’s consent record

This isn’t a fault — the two systems were simply built to run on their own.

Consent × Email 02 · 04

What happens to “do not solicit”

The flag keeps most clients out — but not the ones already on a list

A “do not solicit” flag filters most of these clients off send lists in the first place. But for the ones who do reach a list, the flag doesn’t stop the email — most of them are sent to anyway.

Upstream

Before a client reaches a send list: the flag works. About 70% are filtered out.

Downstream

Once a client is on a send list: the flag doesn’t stop the send.

~9,500

do-not-solicit clients emailed in a single quarter — of the ~15,000 who reached a send list (the other ~70% were correctly kept off)

The safeguard holds at the front door, not at the moment of sending.

Consent × Email 03 · 04

Reading the consent switches correctly

Not every “no” means “don’t email me”

One switch that looks like an email opt-out — “Share for Marketing across RBC” — actually controls whether a client’s data can be shared across RBC entities. It is not an email setting. A client can be “No” on sharing and still be perfectly emailable.

Looks like an email opt-out (but isn’t) Share for Marketing across RBC
Actually controls email Do-not-solicit Email-channel consent
61%

of clients set to “No” on the sharing switch still received email — more than those set to “Yes” (32%). It isn’t an email control.

The real email gates are the do-not-solicit and email-channel switches — read those.

Consent × Email 04 · 04

Where this leaves us

No closed loop between consent and send — and the fix is already being built

Across all of this, one pattern holds: consent decisions don’t reliably reach the send system. The encouraging part — a project is already underway to build that missing link.

MTEC-12644 Real-time CPC Unsubscribe

What it should connect

  • Unsubscribes flowing back to the consent record
  • A consent check closer to the moment of sending, for do-not-solicit clients
  • Mail sent outside the campaign taxonomy, which today isn’t covered by campaign-level suppression

What we need from the channel owners

  • Confirm how consent is applied to your sends today
  • Confirm nothing currently syncs the two systems
  • Confirm MTEC-12644’s scope covers the gaps above
