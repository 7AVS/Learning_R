PROMPT FOR CLAUDE DESIGN - BUILD A POWERPOINT DECK

Build a PowerPoint deck from the content below. This prompt is fully self-contained - narrative, data, slide specs, and style rules are all included. Do not invent numbers, categories, or claims beyond what is given here.

===========================================================
1. CONTEXT
===========================================================
This is for the Cards pod measurement team (marketing analytics, a bank NBA/campaign team) at the quarterly "Power Pack" venue. Audience: marketing executives - non-statistical, time-constrained. This is a findings spotlight on email-consent infrastructure: a data quality/compliance-adjacent discovery about how the bank tracks (or fails to track) client email opt-outs, surfaced as a byproduct of unrelated measurement work.

Hard tone rules - follow exactly:
- Findings, not recommendations. State what the data shows. Do not tell the audience what to do about it, do not propose a fix, do not editorialize on urgency.
- No hedge language. No "may," "could potentially," "it seems," "roughly speaking" as filler. State numbers and facts plainly. (Ranges/percentages that are genuinely approximate should still be stated as approximate - e.g. "~35x" - but do not wrap the whole claim in hedging.)
- No marketing fluff. No adjectives like "exciting," "powerful," "game-changing." This is a plain factual finding for executives.
- Never presuppose design intent. Do not imply CPC or any system "should" have caught, gated, or blocked what it didn't. State the observation, not an assumed failure against an unbuilt or undocumented design. Avoid phrasing that bakes in a design assumption (e.g. do not say a consent flag "should" translate into delivery, or frame a gap as a "failure" of an integration that was never confirmed to exist).
- Every number on every slide must be traceable to a source shown in the footnote on that slide. Do not round in a way that changes meaning; do not fabricate any figure not given here.
- One claim per slide. Each slide states a single finding; supporting numbers/visuals serve that one claim.
- Internal mnemonics and system codes (CPC, SFMC, MNE codes, switch numbers, TACTIC codes) are fine to show. No client-identifying data of any kind - every number here is an aggregate client count, keep it that way.

2. THE STORY - six content slides
===========================================================

--- SLIDE 1: "Email consent: what the data shows" (title / context) ---

Purpose: orient the audience before any number appears. No findings on this slide - universe and window only.

Headline (verbatim, H1): "Email consent: what the data shows"

One-line universe (required, under the headline): Cards pod measurement, window Jul 2025-Jun 2026. Universe is NBA campaign email (SFMC feed) against CPC preference-log switches 1002, 1012, and 1014 - the 3 email-relevant of roughly 40 preference codes in the CPC preference dictionary.

Small print (method line, bottom of slide): Two independent record systems, compared over the same window. No number in this deck implies a causal link between an individual unsubscribe and an individual CPC event unless stated on that slide.

No stat callouts on this slide. No chart. Text only.

--- SLIDE 2: "Two record systems of very different scope" (scope contrast) ---

Narrative: The bank records email consent in two disconnected places. The email vendor world (SFMC - Salesforce Marketing Cloud) logs unsubscribes at a much higher volume than the bank's own consent gate (CPC - the bank's client preference/consent system) logs opt-outs. Across the three CPC switches relevant to this comparison, CPC logs roughly 750 opt-outs/month against roughly 26,600 vendor unsubscribes/month - a gap of about 35x.

Framing line (required, place directly under the headline, smaller type - this is not optional supporting text, it is the honesty check on the whole slide): a one-click email unsubscribe and a deliberate preference-centre or assisted-channel action carry different friction. The volumes compared on this slide measure that difference in behavior between two channels, not one system failing relative to the other.

Secondary fact (supporting text, smaller than the headline - "the writers line"): CPC opt-outs are written overwhelmingly through assisted channels - branch and contact centre. The vendor's own automated pipe into CPC writes an estimated 15-16 clients per year, through switch 1012 only.

Headline stat callout: "35x" (hero-number tile) - vendor unsubscribes outnumber CPC opt-outs by roughly 35 times, per month, across the three relevant switches. Mini stat cells beside it: 26,647 avg email unsubscribes/mo; 754 avg CPC opt-outs/mo.

Visual: two small-multiple bar charts stacked side by side, one per series (email unsubscribes; CPC opt-outs), sharing a month axis, Jul 2025-Jun 2026. Do not force both series onto one shared linear axis - the CPC series is two orders of magnitude smaller and would be invisible.

Data for this chart: see appendix 3.1.

Footnote (methodology, small type): E1 - two independent event streams over the same months; no client-level link between an individual unsubscribe and an individual CPC opt-out is implied.
Source line: Evidence 1, 3.

--- SLIDE 3: "The two systems share almost nothing" (the blind gate) ---

Narrative: Of 319,733 clients who unsubscribed from the vendor between Jul 2025 and Jun 2026, only 1,387 (0.4%) hold an explicit CPC opt-out, at any time, before or after the unsubscribe (the most generous read available for CPC). Of those 1,387: 1,252 were recorded BEFORE the client's unsubscribe (already standing opted-out in CPC, then unsubscribed at the vendor - not evidence the unsub reached CPC); 135 were recorded AFTER (the outer bound on anything that could reflect the unsub propagating into CPC).

Headline (verbatim, H1): "The two systems share almost nothing"

Visual: flow diagram - box "319,733 clients unsubscribed (Jul 2025-Jun 2026)" -> arrow with an X/break marker, labeled "0.4% carries through" -> box "1,387 hold an explicit CPC opt-out (0.4%)". Below the diagram, a small before/after split shown as two stat cells: 1,252 (before) / 135 (after).

Primary callout (large numeral, this slide's headline stat): "135" - labeled "clients/yr - the propagation ceiling: the most that any unsub-to-CPC event could reflect the unsub itself (0.04% of unsubscribers)."

Supporting text (small, below the callout): The 1,252 "before" group are clients who were already standing opted-out in CPC, received campaign mail, and later unsubscribed at the vendor - not evidence that the unsub reached CPC.

Panel title convention: state the comparison, not an implied failure of intent (e.g. "Vendor unsubscribe -> CPC consent record" - not "CPC fails to catch unsubscribes").

Footnote (methodology, small type): Consent standing = latest answer recorded at any point in time, before or after the unsubscribe - the most generous read available for CPC.
Source line: Evidence 2.

--- SLIDE 4: "Unsubscribing does not silence the channel" (the leaking channel) ---

Narrative: Of 248,130 clients who unsubscribed from the vendor between Jul 2025 and Mar 2026, 25,721 (10.4%) received a campaign email in the following quarter (Apr-Jun 2026).

Headline (verbatim, H1): "Unsubscribing does not silence the channel"

Visual: flow diagram - box "248,130 clients unsubscribed (Jul 2025-Mar 2026)" -> arrow with an X/break marker, labeled "10.4% received email" -> box "25,721 received campaign email (10.4%), Apr-Jun 2026". Annotate the arrow neutrally - state the rate, not a judgment ("received email," not "still gets email anyway").

Candidate mechanisms: list as open questions, not claims, visually flagged as pending/unresolved (dashed border, muted styling) - program-level suppression scope; multiple email addresses per client; re-consent between the unsub and Apr 2026. Do not present these as findings; they are unconfirmed hypotheses for the gap, not established causes.

Footnote (methodology, small type): Sequenced - unsubscribed before Apr 1, 2026 -> campaign email received Apr-Jun 2026 (3-month window). Fixed pre/post window, not a matched-pair test.
Source line: Evidence 5.

--- SLIDE 5: "Do-not-solicit clients receive named campaigns" (the do-not-solicit leak, by campaign) ---

Narrative: The slide's LEAD number is the entity do-not-solicit finding (switch 1002) - 1002 is the one switch whose meaning is unambiguous ("do not solicit"), so it anchors the headline. Of 49,444 clients standing do-not-solicit (switch 1002) before Apr 1, 2026, 9,496 (19.2%) received a campaign email in the following quarter (Apr-Jun 2026).

Headline (verbatim, H1): "Do-not-solicit clients receive named campaigns"

Primary callout (hero number): "19.2%" - labeled "of clients standing do-not-solicit (1002) before Apr 1, 2026 received campaign email Apr-Jun 2026." Mini stat cells beside it: 49,444 (standing do-not-solicit, before Apr 1); 9,496 (received campaign email, Apr-Jun).

Visual: bar chart, top 12 campaigns (by mne code) reaching this cohort, Apr-Jun 2026, sorted descending by clients. Data: see appendix 3.3a.

Supporting line: every row is a named campaign deployment, not transactional mail. Emphasize (bold inline, not a separate callout) the four Cards pod campaigns reaching this cohort even where they fall outside the visible top-12: PCD 697, PCL 691, PCQ 525, AUH 518.

Demoted secondary line (visually smaller/muted, NOT the same weight or color as the 19.2% primary callout): "29.4% across 1002/1012/1014 combined (96,317 clients); 47% among the 1012-only exclusive cohort (13,214 clients) - shown for scale, not weighted the same as the 1002 finding above." Followed by a distinct pending/unresolved note (dashed border, muted styling): "Switch mapping: 1012 (banking email) and 1014 (marketing sharing) pending confirmation with system owners."

Footnote (methodology, small type): Cohort - latest 1002 (entity do-not-solicit) standing before Apr 1, 2026. Sends - campaign email Apr-Jun 2026, by mne = SUBSTR(TREATMENT_ID, 8, 3).
Source line: Evidence 4, 6.

--- SLIDE 6: "The system view" (closing) ---

Narrative: this slide synthesizes slides 2-5 into one chain - it introduces no new numbers, only restates the three leak rates already shown, plus the one delivery-side open item.

Headline (verbatim, H1): "The system view"

Visual: three stacked flow rows (same box-arrow-box grammar as slides 3-4, at reduced scale):
 1. "Vendor unsub" -> (10.4%) -> "Continued campaign mail"
 2. "Vendor unsub" -> (0.04%) -> "CPC consent record"
 3. "CPC do-not-solicit" -> (19.2%) -> "Campaign delivery"

One-sentence synthesis (bold, prominent, this is the slide's single claim): "No single system in the chain treats no as no - and the systems do not talk to each other."

Coverage strip: "MTEC-12644 (blueprint, Jun 2026) addresses unsub -> consent-record write-back only. The delivery side of the chain is a confirmation question to system owners."

Confirmation questions (list exactly 3, styled as pending/unresolved - these are open questions to system owners, not findings):
 1. Which suppression list does send-time read from?
 2. Which switch is the lawful campaign-email key, and what is its SLA?
 3. Does program-level express consent override a standing opt-out?

Do not say CPC or any system "fails" or is "broken" anywhere on this slide. "Does not talk to each other" and "does not treat no as no" are the strongest phrasing allowed.

Footnote (methodology, small type): Chain restates the rates from Evidence 2, 4, and 5; not independently recomputed here.
Source line: Evidence 2, 4, 5; MTEC-12644 detail - cpc_evidence_dossier.html section 7.

===========================================================
Carry these statements as small-type footnotes on the slides indicated. Do not fold them into body copy or headline text - they are methodology context, not findings.

- Universe (Slide 1): NBA campaign email (SFMC feed) vs CPC switches 1002/1012/1014 (entity do-not-solicit, banking email, marketing sharing - the 3 email-relevant of ~40 preference codes).

- E4/E6 cohort + switch scope (Slide 5): sequenced - opted-out standing before Apr 1, 2026 -> campaign email received Apr-Jun 2026. 1002 = entity do-not-solicit (meaning unambiguous); 1012/1014 shown for scale, pending switch-mapping confirmation with system owners - not presented as breaches, and not the slide's lead number.

5. FORMAT
===========================================================
- 16:9 widescreen.
- Structure: six slides, in the order given above (title/context, then the five findings/closing slides). Do not add extra content slides beyond these six; do not cut any of the six for length - none is filler.
- Executive-clean layout: generous white space, minimal on-slide text, one clear takeaway per slide expressed visually first, text second.
- Prefer charts/diagrams over tables wherever the data supports it. The 7-row cohort breakdown (appendix 3.3) is condensed into the two headline numbers on Slide 5 (49,444 -> 9,496) plus the demoted 29.4%/47% line - do not reproduce all 7 rows as a slide table. The 18-row campaign list (appendix 3.3a) becomes a top-12 bar chart plus a 4-item Cards-pod callout - do not reproduce all 18 rows.
- The headline numbers per slide - 35x (Slide 2); 135 / propagation ceiling (Slide 3); 10.4% (Slide 4); 19.2% primary, 29.4%/47% demoted (Slide 5) - must appear as prominent stat callouts (big numeral, short label underneath), not just inline in sentences. Size and color must visually rank each slide's primary number above any demoted/secondary line on the same slide.
- Open/unresolved items (Slide 4's candidate mechanisms, Slide 5's switch-mapping caveat, Slide 6's confirmation questions) get distinct pending/unresolved styling (e.g. dashed border, muted color) - visually distinguishable from the resolved, footnoted findings.
- Visual identity: match official RBC investor-deck styling as the design benchmark (see internal reference: RBC Q2 2026 Investor Presentation slide styling) - a clean corporate navy/blue-and-white palette with a single restrained accent color, sans-serif type hierarchy (large bold numerals for stat callouts, clean body sans-serif for supporting text), minimal chart chrome (no heavy gridlines, no 3D effects, no default-software chart styling), generous margins, consistent slide numbering. If exact RBC brand hex values are not available to you, default to a professional navy/dark-blue + white + single neutral-accent palette rather than generic bright presentation-software colors - do not invent an RBC brand color and label it as official.
