===========================================================
1. CONTEXT
===========================================================
This is for the Cards pod measurement team (marketing analytics, a bank NBA/campaign team) at the quarterly "Power Pack" venue. Audience: marketing executives — non-statistical, time-constrained. This is a findings spotlight on email-consent infrastructure: a data quality/compliance-adjacent discovery about how the bank tracks (or fails to track) client email opt-outs, surfaced as a byproduct of unrelated measurement work.

Hard tone rules — follow exactly:
- Findings, not recommendations. State what the data shows. Do not tell the audience what to do about it, do not propose a fix, do not editorialize on urgency.
- No hedge language. No "may," "could potentially," "it seems," "roughly speaking" as filler. State numbers and facts plainly. (Ranges/percentages that are genuinely approximate should still be stated as approximate — e.g. "~35x" — but do not wrap the whole claim in hedging.)
- No marketing fluff. No adjectives like "exciting," "powerful," "game-changing." This is a plain factual finding for executives.
- Every number on every slide must be traceable to a source shown in the appendix/footnote below. Do not round in a way that changes meaning; do not fabricate any figure not given here.
- Internal mnemonics and system codes (CPC, SFMC, MNE codes, switch numbers, TACTIC codes) are fine to show. No client-identifying data of any kind — every number here is an aggregate client count, keep it that way.

2. THE STORY — two content slides
===========================================================

--- SLIDE 1: "Email consent lives in two record systems of very different scope" (zoom-out) ---

Narrative: The bank records email consent in two disconnected places. The email vendor world (SFMC — Salesforce Marketing Cloud) logs unsubscribes at a much higher volume than the bank's own consent gate (CPC — the bank's client preference/consent system) logs opt-outs. Across the three CPC switches relevant to this comparison, CPC logs roughly 750 opt-outs/month against roughly 26,000 vendor unsubscribes/month — a gap of about 35x.

Framing line (required, place directly under the headline, smaller type - this is not optional supporting text, it is the honesty check on the whole slide): a one-click email unsubscribe and a deliberate preference-centre or assisted-channel action carry different friction. The volumes compared on this slide measure that difference in behavior between two channels, not one system failing relative to the other. Do not let the headline or the chart framing imply that CPC "should" be catching these unsubscribes - the slide is a scope contrast, not a failure claim.

Secondary fact (include as supporting text, smaller than the headline - "the writers line"): CPC opt-outs are overwhelmingly written by assisted human channels — branch platform (system code 7001) and contact centre (system code 7003). The email vendor's own pipe into CPC writes only about 15 clients per year, and only through one switch (1012).

Headline stat callout: "~35x" — vendor unsubscribes outnumber CPC opt-outs by roughly 35 times, per month, across the three relevant switches. Keep this number and the small-multiple charts - only the framing line above is new.

Suggested visual: a monthly dual-series chart (Jul 2025–Jun 2026) — either two lines on a dual y-axis (CPC opt-outs is two orders of magnitude smaller than email unsubs, so a single shared axis will flatten the CPC line) or two small-multiple bar charts stacked vertically, one per series, sharing a month axis. Place the "~35x" stat callout as a standalone large-number tile beside or below the chart, not embedded inside it. Do not attempt to force both series onto one linear axis without a dual scale — the CPC series would be invisible.

Data for this chart (see appendix section 3.1 for the verbatim monthly series).

2A. METHODOLOGY (must appear as footnotes on slides)
===========================================================
Carry these four statements as small-type footnotes on the slides indicated. Do not fold them into body copy or headline text - they are methodology context, not findings.

- Universe (Slide 1 and Slide 2): Universe is NBA campaign email (SFMC vendor feed) vs CPC switches 1002/1012/1014 (entity do-not-solicit, banking email, marketing sharing - the 3 email-relevant of ~40 preference codes).
- E1 independence (Slide 1): The two series (vendor unsubscribes, CPC opt-outs) are two independent event streams over the same months. No linkage between an individual unsubscribe and an individual CPC opt-out is implied by this chart.
- E2 any-time-generous + before/after split (Slide 2, Direction 1 panel): Consent standing = the latest answer recorded at any point in time, before or after the client's unsubscribe - the most generous read available for CPC. A before/after split (was the qualifying CPC opt-out recorded before or after the client's first unsubscribe) exists in the underlying query; see appendix 3.2 for the current shape and status.
- E4 sequenced window (Slide 2, Panel B): Sequenced - opted-out standing before Apr 1, 2026 -> campaign email received Apr-Jun 2026 (3-month window). This is a fixed pre/post window, not a matched-pair test.
- Switch scope (Slide 2, Panel B): 1002 = entity do-not-solicit (meaning unambiguous); 1012/1014 shown for scale pending switch-mapping confirmation with system owners - not presented as breaches, and not the slide's lead number.
