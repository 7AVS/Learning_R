Exploratory analysis · what we studied

The relationship between an email unsubscribe and the bank's consent record (CPC)

The question

When a client unsubscribes from a marketing email, what does their consent state in the Consent Preference Centre look like, and does the unsubscribe relate to it?

An exploratory read. We describe what we monitored and what we observed.

The two data sources

Vendor email feedback (SFMC): every NBA marketing-campaign email unsubscribe, across roughly 30 active programs. Grain = email address.

CPC preference log: an event log — one row each time a client's consent switch is written. Grain = client × switch × time.

The switches we monitor

1002Do-not-solicit (RBC Royal Bank). Blank counts as Yes.
1012Banking e-mail — email channel consent. Blank counts as Yes.
1014Share-for-Marketing across RBC — read by campaign decisioning to select clients. Blank counts as No.
Consent states: 5001 Yes · 5002 No · 5003 blank. On 1014 an explicit No and a blank are one population and are counted together throughout. A client with no row at all has no consent record and is not counted either way.

How we connect them

We follow the same clients. Of 319,733 who unsubscribed between July 2025 and June 2026, we join each on client number into their CPC records — every write, and their standing position.

Standing position = the client's most recent write on that switch. Email-to-client join: 1.12 addresses per client.

Where the blank comes from

One system wrote 3,556,897 of the 3,557,099 blanks on 1014, and it is still writing them today

What we counted

Every client's current position on 1014, and the system that wrote it.

Of the 3,557,099 standing blanks, 3,556,897 were written by system 7999 — 99.99%. Four other systems account for 202 between them.

7999 also holds 91% of the explicit Yes positions. Explicit No comes from elsewhere: branch (7001) 35,393, and two undocumented codes, 7033 and 7058, at 19,639 and 19,410.

On 1012 — the switch clients are asked — 7999 writes nothing at all.

7999 is identified in the preference dictionary as the onboarding and default batch. Codes 7033, 7053 and 7058 appear at volume and are not in the dictionary, which ends at 7030.

Timing · when a CPC write follows an unsubscribe

Of the 135 explicit opt-outs recorded after an unsubscribe, 58 were written more than ninety days later

What we counted

Of the 319,733 unsubscribers, 1,387 hold an explicit opt-out on a monitored switch. 1,252 were already recorded before the client unsubscribed; 135 after.

The chart shows how long after the unsubscribe those 135 were written: 21 within the first week, 58 beyond ninety days.

The same test on blank writes returns two clients on each switch, every one written by 7999.
