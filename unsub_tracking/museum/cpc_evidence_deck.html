The relationship between an email unsubscribe and the bank's consent record (CPC)

The question

When a client unsubscribes from a marketing email, does that action reach — or relate to — their consent state in the bank's Consent Preference Centre (CPC)?

An initial, exploratory read: we describe what we monitored and observed, and leave the conclusions to the reader.

The two data sources

Vendor email feedback (SFMC): every NBA marketing-campaign email unsubscribe — a one-click "unsubscribe" (disposition 4), across ~30 active programs. Grain = email address.

CPC preference log: each client's consent switches and how they change. Grain = client × switch.

The switches we monitor

1002Do-not-solicit (RBC Royal Bank) — marketing gate. Dictionary-confirmed.
1012Banking e-mail — email channel consent. meaning owner-confirm pending
1014Share-for-Marketing across RBC — cross-entity data sharing, not email. owner-confirm pending
Sharing family also: 1015 (services), 1016 (bank & credit-bureau), 1036 (online-personalization). Consent states: 5001 Yes · 5002 No · 5003 blank. Policy treats blank as No on 1014/1015 for sharing; for measurement we keep blank separate (blank ≠ an answered No).

How we connect them (method)

We follow the same clients. Of 319,733 who unsubscribed (Jul 2025 – Jun 2026), we join each on client number into their CPC records — any change within 90 days, and their latest standing.

Email-to-client join: 1.12 addresses per client; the reverse risk (one address → several clients) isn't yet quantified. Volume comparison (next slide) is two streams, not yet linked.

Zoom out · two streams, not yet linked

What we see first: email unsubscribes and CPC opt-outs are very different-sized streams

What we counted

Email unsubscribes — clients by the month of their first unsubscribe (code 4): about 27,000 a month.

CPC opt-outs — clients with a "No" (5002) flip on a monitored switch: about 800 a month.

Two differently-sized streams, roughly 35× apart on the 12-month average — so even if every CPC opt-out were an unsubscriber, at most ~3% of monthly unsubs could be reaching CPC. The next slides ask whether the same clients connect.

Volume only — no client-level link here, by design; each is against a different population at risk. Opt-out volume counts explicit-No on the 3 monitored switches (1002/1012/1014); the journey (slide 3) scans all switches.

Zoom in · does an unsubscribe trigger a CPC change?

What we observed: CPC changes after an unsubscribe are rare and human-written — no automated path

What we tested

Within 90 days of an unsubscribe, only 0.28% of clients show any CPC No/blank change (0.3% on clients with a full 90-day window, so it isn't a censoring effect).

Those few writes are branch / onboarding activity on sharing switches (1016/1036), not the email switches — the email platform reached just 19 clients (a low-volume SFMC path, scope to confirm). For context, the same clients' prior-90-day rate was 1.99%, but that pre-window likely reflects onboarding writes, so we don't lean on it.

No sign of an automated path from an unsubscribe into the consent record.

"CPC No/blank change" = a new No or blank state written on any switch within 90 days of the unsubscribe (opt-ins excluded).

Zoom in · how each switch relates to email — explicit answer vs never-asked

What we see: only do-not-solicit (1002) clearly tracks email; the others are weak or flat

What we compared

Share who received email in Apr–Jun, split by explicit answer (not lumping in blanks).

1002 (do-not-solicit): an explicit No is emailed far less than Yes (19% vs 45%). 1012: No and Yes are the same (30% vs 29%). 1014 (sharing): No is only mildly below Yes (26% vs 32%).

The never-asked blank majority is heavily emailed on 1002 and 1014 (60%+); on 1012 almost everyone is explicitly set (only 326 blanks). An earlier read that folded blanks into "No" overstated 1014 — corrected here.

1002 is the switch that tracks email; 1012 and 1014 do not clearly gate it.

The 1002 No/Yes gap may partly reflect list composition, not only enforcement (a slide-6 ask); switch populations differ, and 1012 covers a smaller base.

Zoom in · do-not-solicit clients who receive marketing email

What we see: a small but persistent share of do-not-solicit clients receive marketing email

What we counted

49,446 clients stand No on 1002. About 70% (34,430) have no send record; 15,016 do, and 9,498 received marketing email in Apr–Jun.

It is persistent, not a timing effect: the rate holds at 19–22% whether the No was set last month or over two years ago; only 49 clients flipped back to Yes in the quarter; and the sends span many campaigns (codes QCF, VRE, Cards' PCD/PCL, and untagged "DEFAULT" mail).

A standing divergence between the 1002 flag and observed sends — cause not yet confirmed (see the slide-6 ask on how 1002 is consumed at send time).

Sends = ~30 marketing campaigns in the vendor feed; "in the send system" = a vendor record in the trailing ~14 months (receipt window Apr–Jun 2026, standing 1 Apr). Candidate causes of the divergence: 1002's entity scope, the untagged DEFAULT stream, or the downstream suppression basis. Open item: the 34,430 with no send record aren't yet split by email-on-file / dormant / suppressed — tracked to-do (needs a client-attribute table).

What the analysis opens

  • CPC changes after an unsubscribe are rare (0.3%) and branch/onboarding-written; the email platform reached just 19 clients — no systematic path
  • Of the monitored switches, only 1002 clearly tracks email; 1012 and 1014 do not
  • A persistent ~19% of do-not-solicit clients receive marketing email — unexplained

What we need from the channel owners

  • Confirm the meaning of 1012 and 1014 and how each is applied to sends
  • Confirm how 1002 is consumed at send time, and where in the selection-to-send cycle
  • Confirm the CPC writer-code taxonomy — could a sync write under a non-ESP code?
  • Confirm whether anything currently syncs the two systems, and MTEC-12644's scope
