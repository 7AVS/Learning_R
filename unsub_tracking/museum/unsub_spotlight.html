-- EXPOSED: mailed by the campaign, left anywhere in the window
SELECT s.mne,
       COUNT(DISTINCT s.CLNT_NO)                                   AS mailed,
       COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN c.bucket='leaver' THEN s.CLNT_NO END) AS leavers_exposed
FROM cards_send s JOIN clients c ON c.CLNT_NO = s.CLNT_NO
GROUP BY 1;

-- ATTRIBUTED: the mnemonic on the client's OWN unsubscribe event
SELECT SUBSTR(TREATMENT_ID,8,3) AS mne, COUNT(DISTINCT CLNT_NO) AS unsubs_attributed
FROM DTZV01.VENDOR_FEEDBACK_EVENT
WHERE disposition_cd = 4
  AND disposition_dt_tm >= DATE '2026-03-01' AND disposition_dt_tm < DATE '2026-06-01'
GROUP BY 1;

-- per 1,000 = 1000.0 * leavers / mailed
Caveats
  • CRV has no attributed figure. It did not reach L6's top-25-by-senders cut, so the number was never captured. Blank is honest; carrying exposure across would not be.
  • Mailed counts are distinct clients, not sends. A client mailed four times by PCL counts once.
  • Rates are within-window. A campaign that fired once in March is compared against one that fired weekly.

BRIEF 3bHow much mail, and does more of it cost us

Unsubscribe rate by number of emails received in the window. The answer is not a straight line.

The rate peaks at 7–12 emails (0.887%) and then falls to 0.526% at 13+. The most-mailed group is the safest group. The straightforward reading is survivorship — people who tolerate thirteen emails in three months have already self-selected as people who do not unsubscribe. It is not evidence that more mail is safe; it is evidence that the heaviest-mailed segment is not a random one.

-- band each client by sends received in the window, then rate within band
WITH b AS (
  SELECT CLNT_NO, bucket,
         CASE WHEN n_send_events <= 1  THEN '1 email'
              WHEN n_send_events <= 3  THEN '2-3 emails'
              WHEN n_send_events <= 6  THEN '4-6 emails'
              WHEN n_send_events <= 12 THEN '7-12 emails'
              ELSE '13+ emails' END AS contact_band
  FROM clients)
SELECT contact_band,
       SUM(CASE WHEN bucket='leaver' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS leavers,
       SUM(CASE WHEN bucket='stayer' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS stayers,
       100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN bucket='leaver' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
             / (SUM(CASE WHEN bucket IN ('leaver','stayer') THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)) AS unsub_rate_pct
FROM b GROUP BY 1;
-- already_out is excluded from both sides: those clients opted out BEFORE the window
-- and were never at risk of leaving during it.
Caveats
  • Derived, not directly transcribed. Built by summing the six age rows inside each contact band of L10b. The five bands reconcile to 62,658 leavers and 9,072,977 stayers — L7's totals to the digit. If it had not reconciled this chart would not exist.
  • Sends are counted bank-wide, not per campaign. A client's twelfth email may be from any of ~200 mnemonics.
  • Regulatory mail is included in these counts. 22 mnemonics carry regulatory content and cannot be opted out of; excluding them shifts clients down a band. Correction pending the re-run.

BRIEF 4 — PARTIALAccounts held. Not depth of relationship.

Ratio by number of accounts held across the four TIBC categories. 1.00 = the average client. Read the orange box before quoting any of it.

This chart does not answer brief item 4, and I mislabelled it. It bands on prod_cnt, which sums the four TIBC account counts — so a client with three chequing accounts scores 3, identical to a client holding chequing + investment + mortgage. One of those is a deep relationship and the other is not. TIBC = Transactional, Investment, Borrower, Cards — the four UCP product categories. The brief asks for buckets 1 through 4 because there are exactly four categories, so the measure it wants is categories held, not accounts summed. Now coded as depth_band (BAND_VERSION 4) and correct on the next run.

The one thing here that survives the mislabelling is the cards-vs-bank split, because it compares like with like. A one-account client is 1.32× over-represented among AUH's leavers and 0.78× among CRV's. The bank number, 0.91, is those two opposite behaviours cancelling. Whatever the band is called, the bank average hides a real disagreement between two cards campaigns — which is the argument for a cards lens.

-- ratio = share of leavers in the band  ÷  share of stayers in the band
WITH s AS (
  SELECT prod_band,
         SUM(CASE WHEN bucket='leaver' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS lv,
         SUM(CASE WHEN bucket='stayer' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS st
  FROM banded GROUP BY 1)
SELECT prod_band, lv, st,
       (1.0*lv/SUM(lv) OVER ()) / (1.0*st/SUM(st) OVER ()) AS ratio
FROM s;
-- the CARDS version runs the identical expression over cards_send, so each campaign
-- is measured against ITS OWN mailed base, not against the bank.
Caveats
  • Only AUH and CRV have a cards cut. PCL, PCQ and PCD sit in csv_l9_per_campaign_ratios (111 rows) on HDFS, never fetched. Two campaigns is not "cards" — it is two campaigns.
  • Product counts come from the UCP snapshot at 2026-02-28, one common pre-window anchor. Clients acquired during Mar–May cannot appear in it and are counted as unmatched, not as zero-product.
  • A ratio is not a rate. 1.32 means over-represented among leavers relative to that campaign's own base — not that 32% more clients left.

FINDINGAge is the one lever that survives a control

Unsubscribe rate by age, bank and cards. Dashed line = bank-wide 0.686%.

Bank Cards campaign

Under-25s leave at 0.91% against a bank rate of 0.69%; over-45s at 0.54%. Nearly a 2× spread across age, and it is the widest spread of any attribute in this pack. Inside CRV the young rate is 1.29% — the highest single number here.

The obvious objection: young clients simply get more email. Below, the same rate with mail volume held fixed.

Under-25s are over-represented among leavers in every contact band — 1.63 at one email down to 1.25 at 7–12, never below 1.25. That rules out the obvious objection: it is not that young clients simply receive more mail, because the ratio holds when mail volume is held fixed. Tenure does not survive the same test — 1.54 at one email collapses to 1.04 at 4–6. Do not state the two with equal confidence.

Value is flat and mildly protective. The top profit quintile sits at 0.84 — we are not losing our best clients. That is a null result, and it kills a story the pack might otherwise have told. It is also not independent of age: PROF_TOT_ANNUAL is current-year contribution and rises with tenure.

Populations — who is in the denominator

already_out is excluded from every rate on this page. Those 427,079 clients unsubscribed before the window opened and were mailed anyway. They were never at risk of leaving during the window, so including them in the denominator would push every rate down. They are a separate finding, not a baseline.
