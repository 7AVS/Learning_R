Recorded consent position and subsequent campaign email — what the two records contain

What we examined

319,733 clients recorded an email unsubscribe between July 2025 and June 2026, across 194 campaign mnemonics in the vendor feed.

We joined them on client number into the CPC preference log — 13,831,133 rows across four switches, earliest write 2003 — and then forward into who received campaign email in April–June 2026.

Every figure reconciles to its control total: the monthly unsubscribe series sums to 319,733; each standing-position bar sums to that switch’s client count; the 1014 blank-by-year table sums to 3,557,099.

Vocabulary — held consistent throughout

UnsubscribeA client action in the email channel, recorded by the vendor against one campaign. Programme scope not established.
Recorded positionThe value standing on a CPC switch. May be an explicit answer or a value written by a system. We never call it “what the client said”.
StandingThe client’s most recent write on that switch, as at a stated date.
ReceivedA vendor event with disposition code 1 against a campaign carrying a mnemonic — see limits below.

The switches, and what a blank means on each

1002 Do-not-solicit (RBC Royal Bank) · blank counts as Yes  ·  1012 Banking e-mail · blank counts as Yes  ·  1014 Share-for-Marketing, read by campaign decisioning · blank counts as No  ·  1006 carried for comparison only.

States: 5001 Yes · 5002 No · 5003 blank. On 1014, an explicit No and a blank are one population and are counted together throughout. A client with no row at all holds no consent record and is counted in neither direction. No client on 1002, 1012 or 1014 holds a value outside these three.

Four limits to read before the exhibits

Feed scope is not established here. 194 mnemonics is wider than the Cards programme set, and the largest single source — VRE, 116,909 unsubscribes — is not identified in this run. Treat the cohort as the vendor feed, not as Cards mail.

“Received” is a disposition code, not a confirmed delivery. Whether code 1 denotes sent, delivered or rendered is not documented here, and no seed or employee exclusion was applied.

Groups are not mailability-matched. Every unsubscriber is by construction someone we hold an address for and did mail. A CPC population is not. Address coverage per group is not reported in this run.

Four switches only. Any statement about movement into the consent record is bounded by 1002, 1006, 1012 and 1014.

Exhibit 1 of 3
Who received campaign email, by recorded position at 1 April 2026

On 1002, 18.1% of clients with a recorded No received a campaign email in April–June. Of those with a recorded Yes, 44.0% did.

What the data shows

On 1002, of 49,446 clients with a recorded No at 1 April, 8,941 received named campaign email in Apr–Jun (18.1%); of 1,120,205 with a recorded Yes, 492,925 did (44.0%). That is one switch and one population split two ways.

On 1012 the two positions are level and slightly inverted — 28.9% for No against 26.8% for Yes. Separately, of 253,721 clients who unsubscribed before April, 111,651 received such email — 44.0%.

What would have to be ruled out

The unsubscribe bar is not like-for-like with the 1002 bars. Every unsubscriber is someone we hold an address for and did mail; the CPC populations include clients with no address, dormant relationships, and clients in no campaign audience. Restricting all groups to a common mailable base could move the CPC rates in either direction, and this run does not report address coverage.

These figures also cannot separate suppression from composition: clients carrying a recorded No may differ from those carrying a Yes in ways that independently affect whether they are mailed — age of the record, dormancy, closure. No recency or activity cut has been run. Nothing here establishes that a recorded position caused a send or its absence.

What the consent record actually contains

1012 holds 606,261 clients and 326 blanks. The other three hold about 4.1 million each, of which 2.9M, 3.6M and 3.6M are blank.

What the data shows

On 1014, 3,557,099 of 4,182,611 clients holding a record stand at blank. The top writer of those blanks is system 7999, at a share that rounds to 1.000 across 5 writers present; the residual is not separately reported in this run. Blanks continue to accrue — 638,090 written in 2025 and 308,113 in the part-year 2026.

On 1012 there are 326 blanks in total. Its blanks come from a different top writer (7006), and 7999 is active on 1012 — holding 99.2% of that switch’s 572,566 explicit Yes values.

One possible reading

This is consistent with 1012 being a switch clients are actually asked, and the others carrying a value that is set for them. Directionally, it suggests the 1014 “No” population — under the rule that counts blank as No — is largely a defaulted state rather than an expressed preference, which would make it unsuitable as a measure of client intent.

The four switch populations differ in size (606,261 to 4,182,611), so shares are not like-for-like across rows. What 7999 is, and on what trigger it writes, is not established by this data. Standing position uses the latest write with no deterministic tie-break; the number of same-timestamp ties has not been quantified.

Exhibit 3 of 3
The two record systems over the same twelve months

1014 receives about 52,700 writes to “No” a month, of which about 684 are explicit. The monthly count steps down by a third in November.

What the data shows

Across the twelve months, 632,399 client-months were written to No on 1014 — an average of 52,700. Of those, roughly 684 a month were explicit No; the remainder were blanks. Distinct clients unsubscribing averaged 26,644 a month.

The 1014 series steps down between October (65,820) and November (42,903) and has climbed back only partially. The movement sits almost entirely in the blank column; the explicit-No count barely moves.

One possible reading

The two series do not move together, and the 1014 volume is dominated by a component with no obvious relationship to client action. The November step is consistent with a change in the writing process, though nothing here indicates what — a source-system change, a migration or a backfill would all look like this.

The two series sit on different grains and should not be divided into a ratio: 52,700 is client-months, 26,644 is distinct clients. They are plotted together to compare shape and order of magnitude, not to quantify a relationship.

Where this stops
Limits, and what would have to change before anyone acted

What this does not show, and what would make it actionable

What this does not establish

No counterfactual. We cannot say what an unsubscriber would have been mailed at absent the unsubscribe. There is no pre-period baseline in this run.

No mailability match. Restricting every group to clients with a deliverable address could move the CPC rates in either direction. This is the single change most likely to alter the reading.

Programme scope untested. If an unsubscribe is programme-scoped by design, a later send from a different programme is the system working as intended. The 111,651 has not been split by in-programme versus cross-programme.

Timing untested. The cohort pools clients who unsubscribed nine months before the send window with those who left days before it, including any inside the statutory honour window. No cut by age of the unsubscribe has been run.

Record age untested. A recorded No may date from 2003 or from last month. No recency or dormancy cut has been run, so suppression and composition cannot be separated.

Join quality unreported. Everything rests on mapping a vendor email address to a client number. This run does not report the match rate or unmatched volume.

Nothing here is causal. No exhibit shows that a recorded position caused a send or its absence, only that they appear together at different rates.

Open questions for the channel and system owners

  • Which suppression list does send-time read, and from which switch?
  • What does vendor disposition code 1 denote — sent, delivered or rendered — and are seeds and employees in it?
  • Is the vendor feed enterprise-wide or programme-limited, and what is VRE (116,909 unsubscribes, the largest single source)?
  • Is an email unsubscribe intended to be programme-scoped, and is that documented?
  • What is system 7999, and on what trigger does it write a blank to 1014?
  • What are systems 7033, 7053 and 7058? They appear at volume and are not in the preference dictionary, which ends at 7030.
  • What changed in November 2025 that stepped 1014 write volume down by a third?

What would make this actionable

Three things, in order. One: a mailability-matched rerun of Exhibit 1 — same groups, restricted to clients with a send record in the window. Two: the 111,651 split by in-programme versus cross-programme send, which either confirms or removes the strongest alternative explanation. Three: an all-switch check for post-unsubscribe writes, since the present result is bounded by four switches.

Until those exist, this document supports a conversation with the channel and system owners. It does not support a change to any control.
