Cards pod · measurement

Email unsubscribes and the bank’s consent record

All figures cover July 2025 – June 2026. Standing counts are as at 1 April 2026.

Two systems record whether a client wants marketing email. They are maintained separately. This deck sets out what each one contains over the same twelve months, and how much they overlap.

The two data sources

Vendor feedback (SFMC) — one row per email unsubscribe, captured at the moment a client clicks unsubscribe.

CPC preference log — the bank’s client preference record. One row per change to a consent switch, with the system that wrote it.

The three switches we monitor

1002 — RBC do-not-solicit
1012 — banking email consent
1014 — share for marketing across RBC

These are the three email-relevant switches of roughly forty in the preference dictionary.

Slide 5 · overlap

Most unsubscribers already stood “No” on 1014 — by default, not by action

All 319,733 clients who unsubscribed July 2025 – June 2026, against their standing CPC position on switch 1014.

89,518
unsubscribers standing No on 1014 under the rule — 28.0%. Of these, 912 by explicit action and 88,606 by blank default.
6,842
standing an explicit Yes on 1014 — 2.1%. The remaining 223,373 have no row on the switch at all.
1,387
hold an explicit opt-out on any of the three switches — 0.43%. Of those, only 135 were recorded after the unsubscribe.

On 1002 the same 319,733 unsubscribers show 384 explicit No and 223,770 with no row; on 1012, 581 explicit No. Under each switch’s own rule, 319,349 of them read as contactable on 1002 and 319,152 on 1012.

The shape rises with time instead of clustering at the start. An automated hand-off would write within a day or two; 21 of the 135 fall in that range and 58 arrive more than ninety days later. That is the profile of unrelated activity, not a transfer.

The same test on blank writes returns two clients per switch, all written by the onboarding batch. Of the 6,232 explicit No writes on 1014 across the year, 93 followed a prior unsubscribe.
