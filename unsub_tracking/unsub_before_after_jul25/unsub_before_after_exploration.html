What happens after someone unsubscribes?

Every client the bank emailed in July 2025, split into those who unsubscribed that month and those who didn't, then followed for eleven months to June 2026. Two UCP snapshots per client — one at the unsubscribe, one at the end. The companion page asks who leaves; this one asks what it costs.

91.4% matched to the July-2025 UCP snapshot; of those, 358 clients carry a UCP row with no profitability figure. The 630,173 with no UCP row at all are held in a separate band throughout — they are almost entirely outside personal UCP, not a data gap.

1. Unsubscribers leave. In every decile.

Share of each group gone from UCP by June 2026, side by side within each baseline profitability decile. The comparison group is clients emailed the same month who did not unsubscribe.

did not unsubscribe unsubscribed
Ten deciles out of ten, the unsubscribers are gone more often. The gap runs 0.2 to 1.8 points — small in absolute terms, but 20% to 67% higher in relative terms, and it never once reverses. Decile 4 is the widest: 8.61% against 6.85%.

Read the deciles, never the total. Pooled across everyone the numbers say the opposite — 8.41% of unsubscribers gone against 10.90% of the comparison group. That inversion is Simpson's paradox, driven entirely by the no-UCP band, where the comparison group is proportionally larger. The pooled figure is arithmetically true and substantively backwards.

2. Value diverges — but only at the top

Change in annual profitability over the eleven months, unsubscribers minus the comparison group, within each baseline decile. Left of the line means unsubscribers did worse.

unsubscribers fell further behind unsubscribers did better 0 = moved together
Deciles 1 through 6 show nothing. Deciles 7 through 10 show everything. In the bottom six bands the two groups move within a few dollars of each other. In the top four the comparison group holds or grows while the unsubscribers fall — a swing of −$30 to −$73 per client, across 8,047 people.

Why the stratification is not decoration: annual profitability is severely skewed, and unsubscribers sit slightly below the comparison group at baseline — median $119 against $157. Comparing pooled averages would let regression to the mean manufacture a difference on its own. Cutting on the baseline decile is what stops that, and it is why the pooled figure (−$0.61) is not the finding.

3. It is not one campaign doing this

Where the 22,805 unsubscribes came from. VRE alone is 60% of them.

The concentration matters for a different reason than it looks. If VRE carries three in five unsubscribes, then a July-2025 finding is substantially a VRE finding — and whether it generalises to other months and other campaign mixes is untested. SBB is the outlier to ignore: 802 unsubscribers, 0.12% present at follow-up, no baseline profitability at all. Those are business clients, and personal UCP cannot contain them.

4. What would change this reading

Three things, in the order they would bite.

  1. The comparison group is contaminated. Clients who unsubscribed before July 2025 and were emailed anyway are sitting in it, labelled as having chosen not to unsubscribe. They chose nothing — they were already gone. Leaving them in makes the comparison group look worse than it is, which pushes every gap in the optimistic direction. This is measurable and the pull is already written.
  2. One cohort month. July 2025 is a single draw. If its campaign mix was unusual — and with VRE at 60% it may well have been — the top-decile pattern could be a July artifact. Running October and January as separate cohorts is a date change, and three matching results would settle it.
  3. The groups are not mailability-matched. Every unsubscriber is by construction someone we hold an address for and did mail. Restricting both sides to a common reachable base could move the rates in either direction.
What this does not say. Nothing here shows that unsubscribing caused the divergence. Clients who unsubscribe may already have been disengaging for reasons that also reduce their value — the unsubscribe would then be a symptom appearing alongside the decline, not its cause. Separating those requires a pre-period baseline this run does not have.
