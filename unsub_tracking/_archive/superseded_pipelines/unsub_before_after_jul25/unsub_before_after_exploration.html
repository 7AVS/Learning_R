Everything below is cut on the client's profitability decile at July 2025, both groups banded together so a decile means the same dollars on each side. 91.4% matched to that snapshot; the 630,173 with no UCP row sit in their own band and are never pooled in. 1. In the top four deciles the two groups move in opposite directions

Change in annual profitability over the eleven months. Two bars per decile on one zero axis — the clients who stayed subscribed, then the clients who unsubscribed. Right of the line is a gain.

gained over 11 months lost zero
This is a crossing, not a gap. Through decile 6 both bars sit on the same side of zero and differ by a few dollars. From decile 7 they separate and point opposite ways — the stayers gain $20, $32 and $28 while the unsubscribers lose $11, $31 and $45. Decile 10 is the only band where both fall, and the unsubscribers fall four times further.

What would settle it: did the divergence start at the unsubscribe, or was it already underway? A profitability reading from three months before July answers that, and the same two-anchor code reads a third partition without a new pull.

2. Attrition is worse for unsubscribers — by the same flat amount everywhere

How much more of each decile the unsubscribers lost by June 2026, against the clients who stayed subscribed. If unsubscribing cost us the valuable ones, this chart would slope.

Ten deciles out of ten point the same way, and every gap sits between +0.6 and +1.8 points with no trend across the value range. That makes attrition a different phenomenon from the profitability divergence above — one is flat everywhere, the other is nothing until decile 7 and then everything.

Do not quote the pooled figure. Across everyone it reads 8.41% of unsubscribers gone against 10.90% of stayers — the reverse of all ten deciles. The inversion comes from the no-UCP band being proportionally larger on the stayer side. Arithmetically true, substantively backwards.

3. The biggest churn signal here has nothing to do with unsubscribing

Attrition among the clients who stayed subscribed — the comparison group on its own, by profitability decile. No unsubscribes in this chart at all.

Churn peaks at break-even, not at the bottom. Deciles 3, 4 and 5 lose 4.38%, 6.85% and 3.40% of their clients, against 0.71% in the top decile and 1.68% in the lowest. Decile 4's median client is worth −$30 a year. The band straddling zero profitability churns roughly ten times harder than the top — among clients who never touched an unsubscribe link.

The question this raises for the room: is the break-even band a retention problem we have never named, or an artefact of how PROF_TOT_ANNUAL behaves near zero — clients flickering across the boundary rather than genuinely leaving? One deserves a programme; the other is a measurement note.

4. Sixty percent of this cohort came from one campaign

Where the 22,805 July unsubscribes originated, by triggering mnemonic.

With VRE carrying three in five, a July-2025 result is substantially a VRE result. That is a limit on the finding, not a finding about VRE. SBB is different again and should be excluded rather than explained — 802 unsubscribers, 0.12% present at follow-up, no baseline profitability at all. Business clients, and personal UCP cannot hold them.

Cheapest way to know whether this generalises: run October 2025 and January 2026 as their own cohorts. Different campaign mixes, same code, three results instead of one.

5. The comparison group contains people who had already left

No chart — a correction waiting to be applied, not a result.

A client who unsubscribed in March 2025 and was emailed anyway in July is sitting in the comparison group, counted as having chosen not to unsubscribe. They chose nothing — they were already gone. Leaving them in makes the comparison group look worse than it is, which pushes every gap on this page in the optimistic direction. The real divergence is wider than −$73, not narrower.

This is measurable and the pull is already written. Until it runs, read section 1 as a floor rather than an estimate.

What I'd test next

Three things, cheapest first.

  1. Strip the already-gone out of the comparison group. The pull exists; it is a filter plus a rerun of the analysis cells. Widens every gap and removes the strongest objection anyone will raise.
  2. A third anchor, three months before July. Turns "they diverged" into "they diverged starting at the unsubscribe" — or kills the causal reading honestly. Same code, one more partition, no new pull.
  3. Two more cohort months. October and January. If the decile 7–10 crossing repeats on different campaign mixes it is real; if it shows up only in July, it was VRE.
